# Persona model

Interviews vary more by delivery than by content: the same fit question arrives as a friendly aside from a rapport-builder, a time-boxed challenge from a stress interviewer, or a quiet "help me understand" from a skeptical peer. Candidates who rehearsed only content freeze on delivery. Personas exist so the candidate rehearses both.

## How to weight personas

Weighting is a shown judgment, not a hidden score. Work in three passes and display the reasoning in the pack's persona table so the candidate can recalibrate if they know something you don't:

1. **Relationship priors** (table below) set the starting weights.
2. **Interviewer signals** adjust them when a profile exists (rules below).
3. **Company archetype** adjusts them when research reveals the loop structure (see `research-checklist.md` — e.g., a documented values round makes Values/Behavioral guaranteed *somewhere* in the loop even if not in this session).

Depth follows weight: the top persona gets 4–5 questions, mid-weight personas 3, low-weight 2. Every pack still touches all plausible personas — the candidate should never meet a style for the first time live.

## Relationship priors

| Relationship / round | Dominant personas | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recruiter screen | Conversational + light Skeptic | Logistics, motivation-consistency, and knockout checks in a friendly register; the risk is treating it as unscored |
| Hiring manager | Case/Strategist + Skeptic | They're buying capability and derisking gaps; expect live problems on their actual roadmap |
| Peer | Peer Evaluator + Data-Driven Prober | They're pricing what the hire costs and contributes to *their* work; competence probes come collegially |
| Skip-level / executive | Case/Strategist (strategy-heavy) + Pressure | Altitude and composure; time-boxed answers; company-strategy fluency separates candidates here |
| Cross-functional / dedicated values round | Values/Behavioral + Conversational | Scored behavioral conversation, often by someone outside the function; STAR-format stories |
| Technical specialist / bar-raiser-style | Data-Driven Prober + Pressure | Depth verification with deliberate follow-up pressure |
| Panel | Blend the top two personas of each member's relationship | If names are known, assign personas per member in the interviewer read |

## Interviewer-signal adjustments

Apply only public professional signals — profile language, career pattern, published talks and writing. Never infer personality; that's both unreliable and outside this skill's lane.

- Self-described "data-driven", analytics-heavy history → Prober up; every candidate metric will be probed to its assumptions.
- Pricing / finance / monetization career spine → tilt case questions toward pricing, packaging, and unit-economics angles; that's their native language.
- Founder, 0→1, or VC chapters → builder stories land unusually well; expect skepticism about big-company pace, and expect "scrappiness" probes.
- "Customer-obsessed" self-description → customer-first framing in values answers mirrors them; a customer-over-metric story is high-value.
- Long single-company tenure → probes about the candidate's adaptability; many short stints on *their* side is not a signal to use.
- Published talks or posts → their recurring themes show up as questions; read one and note it in the interviewer read.
- The interviewer made a career jump similar to the one the candidate is attempting → flag it: a reverse question about that jump converts the hardest motivation probe into a peer conversation.
- Recent consulting or MBA training → over-signal structure with that interviewer specifically: say the framework aloud before using it, state the hypothesis early. Delivery expectations differ per interviewer even when content doesn't — in multi-interviewer loops, tune delivery per round, not per pack.
- Shared background with an interviewer — language, region, alma mater, community — is never a strategy. Do not coach the candidate to build on it; invoking affinity reads as trading on it and can backfire in either direction. A shared *professional* path (a similar career jump, a comparable domain switch) is fair game; shared identity is not.

## The seven personas

### 1. The Data-Driven Prober
- **Looks like:** reads the CV line by line; asks for inputs, denominators, validation; follow-ups go two levels deeper than the answer.
- **Actually scoring:** whether headline numbers survive scrutiny; whether the candidate knows their own assumptions.
- **Counter-strategy:** know every assumption behind every CV number; name the weakest assumption before they do; volunteer a walkthrough of one analysis end-to-end.
- **Stems:** "Walk me through how you got to that number." / "Which assumption in that model would you defend least?"

### 2. The Pressure / Stress Interviewer
- **Looks like:** interrupts, plays devil's advocate, time-boxes answers, questions motives. Testing composure, not content.
- **Actually scoring:** whether the candidate holds a position without becoming defensive, and concedes uncertainty without folding.
- **Counter-strategy:** pause before answering; restate the question; anchor in data; hold a challenged position through two follow-ups before conceding anything real.
- **Stems:** "Sixty seconds: why you over an internal candidate?" / "That result — how much of it was actually you?"

### 3. The Values / Behavioral Interviewer
- **Looks like:** scenario probes mapped to the company's published values or principles; expects STAR-shaped stories; often from outside the function.
- **Actually scoring:** whether past behavior demonstrates the specific published values — not generic virtue.
- **Counter-strategy:** pre-map one story per published value; "we" for context, "I" for contribution; every harmony story ends with the hard truth actually landing.
- **Stems:** "Tell me about a time you delivered an unpopular truth to leadership." / "When did you put a customer ahead of an internal metric?"

### 4. The Case / Strategist
- **Looks like:** live problems from the actual role — plan this, diagnose that, size this. Scores structure and reasoning over the "right" answer.
- **Actually scoring:** whether the candidate clarifies before structuring, structures before answering, and uses the company's own vocabulary.
- **Counter-strategy:** clarify → objective → segmentation → approach → metrics → risks; think aloud; challenge one premise respectfully per case.
- **Stems:** "Sketch the plan for X — what do you need to know first?" / "Metric Y dropped 20% — diagnose it."

### 5. The Skeptic
- **Looks like:** quiet, persistent probing of the gaps — the background mismatch, the missing competency, the "why leave, why here, why now."
- **Actually scoring:** motivation consistency and honest self-assessment; whether the candidate's story survives cross-examination.
- **Counter-strategy:** rehearse the single most predictable objection until it's boring; adjacent-evidence bridges plus a concrete ramp plan; calibrated candor over bluffing a competency the interviewer personally owns.
- **Stems:** "Which part of this JD would you be weakest at on day one?" / "You've never done X — how will you handle the X in this role?"

### 6. The Conversational / Rapport Builder
- **Looks like:** friendly, unstructured, chatty openers; feels safe. That's the danger — casual-register questions are still scored.
- **Actually scoring:** communication economy, genuine interest, and whatever the JD flags as a plus, asked casually.
- **Counter-strategy:** pre-scripted 90-second arcs for the biography question; treat casual questions about hot JD topics as scored; never answer "I think you've covered everything" to the reverse-question slot.
- **Stems:** "So — walk me through the journey." / "What are you actually playing with in [JD plus-skill] these days?"

### 7. The Peer Evaluator
- **Looks like:** seam-splitting between adjacent teams, "what would you need from me," collaboration scenarios, credit-sharing probes. Objections from other banks return in collegial packaging.
- **Actually scoring:** partnership fit — turf awareness, disagreement style with equals, whether the hire reduces or adds to the peer's load.
- **Counter-strategy:** reciprocity framing (what the candidate brings the peer's team, not just what they need); propose seam *principles* rather than pretending to know the seam; disagreement stories that show spine and tact together.
- **Stems:** "Our teams sit side by side — where's the seam, and what happens when we disagree?" / "How do you handle credit when work spans teams?"

## Protective mini-bank: inappropriate or illegal questions

Include only on the candidate's request. Some interviews drift into questions about age, family plans, health, religion, or immigration status beyond work authorization — improper in many jurisdictions and uncomfortable everywhere. Coach three moves, in escalating order: answer the legitimate underlying concern ("if you're asking about my availability for travel — yes, fully"); redirect to relevance ("happy to talk about anything that bears on the role — on that front…"); and a plain boundary for repeat offenses. Note that specifics vary by jurisdiction, and never coach deception — the candidate declines or redirects, they don't fabricate. A candidate who has rehearsed the redirect once won't freeze on it live.
