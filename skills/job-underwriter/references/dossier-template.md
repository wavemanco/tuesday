# Dossier emission and worked template

The dossier is Tuesday's shared artifact: one file the user carries between skills and sessions. Canonical rules live in `conventions/dossier-schema.md`; this file is the operative copy of what this skill must do, plus a full worked example.

## Emission rules

- **Filename is exactly `tuesday-dossier.md`** — recognizable among a user's uploads months later.
- **Two layers, one artifact.** Concise human-readable report on top; one fenced ```json envelope at the bottom. The report is rendered from the envelope as `dossier-schema.md` §2 defines — two layers that can drift are two artifacts. One envelope entry may render in two places: a `gap_queue` row renders as a queue row *and* as a closing question, from the same `prompt` object. That is one source with two placements, which is not drift; two hand-written copies would be.
- **Offered, never forced.** A one-line offer stands in every delivered assessment, first pass included, immediately before the upgrade menu; the direct offer comes at the natural end of the engagement or whenever the user asks. The user decides whether to carry it.
- **This skill writes its own sections only:** `role_profile`, `evidence_map`, `gap_queue`, `risk_register`, `application_plan`, `evidence_state`, `decision`, `candidate_constraints`, `handoff_to_mock_interview`. It reads but never mutates `pack_feedback` (mock-interview owns it). When updating a dossier that has sections this skill doesn't recognize, carry them through verbatim.
- **Envelope version is `1.2`** (adds `evidence_map[].provenance`; see the canonical schema's version history). Reading a dossier with a higher major version: say so and proceed standalone rather than guessing.
- **Report-only parts, which no envelope field holds:** Evidence status's research line and target table; the upgrade menu; the Role reality rows `role_profile` has no field for (organization, working pattern, scope read, market calibration, contradictions); and the Why, Provenance and Status cells of the application plan (every plan row is proposed until the user approves it, which is engagement state, not evidence). They describe this engagement's research and reading of the role, not the candidate's evidence, and `dossier-schema.md` §2 binds them: carried as the delivered assessment wrote them, read by no later skill, and never a second copy of a fact the envelope holds. Every other line renders from the envelope.
- **The fit ledger renders from `evidence_map`.** Its lines are `evidence_map[].requirement_id`, `priority` and `fit_status`, projected, with a few of `requirement`'s own words beside each row below `direct`, and `decision.summary` does not restate them. A summary that carries its own tally is a third copy of the map, and copies agree with each other more readily than with the map: this file's report line and its summary read "five of seven material requirements `direct`" from v0.1 onward, over a map that had four.
- **`handoff_to_mock_interview` follows the canonical schema, `dossier-schema.md` §6, exactly** — those keys are a contract with another skill, not a suggestion. `uncertain_or_weak_areas` carries only `low`/`none`-confidence requirements; `medium`-confidence claims belong in `verified_candidate_evidence` with their caveat attached. `prohibited_claims` are absolute for every downstream consumer — list every claim this engagement established as un-assertable.

## Worked example

Everything below the rule is one complete `tuesday-dossier.md`, emitted mid-engagement (after one recovery turn) for a fictional candidate and company. It is the reference for tone, length, and structure: the report compresses, the envelope carries everything.

Had the user stated no constraints at all, R8 and R9 would not exist, R1 would be `unclear`, and the line would carry the flag the input contract requires: ``- **Hard gates:** none failed; unconfirmed: R1 (UK work eligibility without sponsorship); candidate constraints not given — checks against them unknown``.

`Reasoning` is a column and not a paragraph underneath. It renders the row spec's
`reasoning` field, which the JSON envelope has always carried and this table used

homeless. What happened next is measurable: `none/low` and `medium (adjacent) /
none (titled)` went into the `Confidence` cell, because a writer who has a nuance
and no cell for it puts the nuance in the nearest cell that will hold text. R3 and
R6 are the rows that would have hedged. Note also that R6 is `direct` fit with a
`presentation` gap: fit status and gap type are separate calls, per
`references/evidence-conventions.md` §3.

| # | Gap (req) | Type | What I currently see | Why it matters | Experience that may count | Useful evidence | Provenance |

| G1 | Community energy knowledge (R4, must_have) | transferability | Confirmed retrofit grants for community buildings; nothing touching community generation, grid connection, or share offers directly | R4 is a `must_have` row at `adjacent`; a stronger bridge narrows the objection most likely to be raised | An applicant's project that included solar panels or a heat network; work alongside a community-benefit society or its share offer; a feasibility study you assessed that raised grid capacity | A sanitized assessment note, a site-visit report, a programme excerpt | `[inferred]` bridge derived from the retrofit grants she described; `[generic]` that this is the likeliest objection |

**The queue holds no questions, and that is the point.** Every row here has a
question worth asking, and until now the row carried it — a `Question:` line in a
prose block sitting fourth of nine sections. Two rules broke on that. The
questions were not the last thing the reader met, which `closing_order` requires
and which graders failed in eight runs of fourteen; and there were two question
sites in one message, which the question-budget rule forbids by name — either the
embedded prompts are the budget or the closing block is, never both. So the row
says what the gap is and what would close it, and **the question itself goes in
the closing block with the others**, tagged with its row id.

The register beneath learned this first and the queue did not, which is the whole
argument in one page: the verification question is a column there because as a
prose line it survived only on entries that arrived with a worked example. Same
failure, same fix, one section apart and four months late.

The verification question is a column for the same reason. Rendered as a prose
line beneath the table it survived only for the entries that arrived with a
worked example attached — which meant the entries carrying the actual red flags,
the ones a candidate most needs a question for, were the ones that lost it. Only
`positive_signal` rows may leave the cell empty, and RR3 fills it anyway because the question is worth asking.

`Hypothesis` is a column on the same argument, and it was missing here for the
same reason `Reasoning` was missing from the evidence map: the JSON envelope has
always carried it and the rendered table dropped it. `Provenance` is beside it
because a hypothesis is a world-claim — about the employer, the market, or how
hiring works — and the first version of this table shipped the hypothesis column
without one. Graders found it immediately: the register's hypotheses were the
unlabeled assertions in the response. The repository ledger had meanwhile claimed
for weeks that this table already carried a Provenance cell, which is the reason
nobody looked. `risk-and-decision.md` sends
a between-two `Severity` call to the hypothesis — "take the lower and put the
reason in the hypothesis" — which is a rule pointing at a cell that was not on the
page. `Severity` reads `—` on `unknown` and `positive_signal` rows because the
field is scoped to risk signals and red flags; `—` is the marker, so that a row
where severity does not apply does not acquire an invented `n/a`, `none`, or a
`low` that reads as a measured judgment.

carries two things at once and loses both: strategy advice is language the
candidate will act on, so it needs a status, and lines like row A2 are claims
about how hiring works, so they need a label. As bullets they read as neutral
counsel and arrive with neither. Every row is proposed — the candidate approves
actions, not just edits — and a row with no provenance is a row asserting
something the analysis cannot source.

## Evidence status

Verified: R1, R2, R3, R4, R6, R8, R9 claims (sources in envelope) — verified is about the claim, not the fit: R4 is verified adjacent experience and its fit stays `adjacent`. Unverified leads: a prior-context mention of a participatory-grantmaking pilot — unconfirmed, prompts a question only if it ever matters. Rejected inferences: "awarded grants under delegated authority", initially inferred from "led the assessment panel" — corrected by user: the panel recommended, the committee decided; recorded so it is never re-derived.

In a host with no search the same rows read `unavailable`, `unavailable`, `n/a`:
the first two naming what they would have moved, which the upgrade menu then
offers, and the third unchanged, because `n/a` and `skipped` are decided from the
inputs and not from the host.

Rows 1 and 2 name every search and fetch the tier line counts; the end-of-programme
report the user linked is the read it names as a user link. This run stopped
at L1: an L2 run adds rows 4 and 5 below row 3, and a search sent outside every
declared target adds an un-numbered `yes` row after them.

That is the target accounting specified in `SKILL.md` Step 2. The dossier carries it because it
renders the same analysis the delivered assessment does — **the spec lives in
Step 2 and not in this file**, because this file is read only when a dossier is
being emitted, and the accounting is owed by every assessment whether one is
emitted or not. `interaction-grammar.md` §4 sends the skip here because a
wasted search and a correctly-skipped one produce identical prose, and an
unfilled row is the only version of that rule anyone can check.

## Upgrade menu

- A sanitized due-diligence checklist, a site-visit report, or any inspectable artifact → R3 to `high`, work sample half-done.
- An answer to G1 → R4's bridge strengthens or resolves.
- The recruiter's answer on contract basis → R8 and RR2 close; decision likely simplifies to `go` or `no_go`.
- The budgeted salary → RR4 closes, and the figure is set against the band in role reality.
- The hiring manager's name → role reality's scope read, and which verified story the application plan leads with for that reader.
- A deeper research pass on Penhallow Foundation (trustee and leadership changes, past grant rounds, staff reports; up to 5 more searches and 2 more fetches) — say the word and it runs.
