Rejected

  • The plan's own won't-build list: hidden text, keyword stuffing, "humanizers"; live interview help, emotion reading, voice cloning; answering consent, attestation or equal-opportunity questions for you; automating LinkedIn, Indeed or Glassdoor; solving CAPTCHAs or disguising the bot; creating job-site accounts or storing passwords; any sending or submitting on your behalf, including the plan's "sanctioned send"; mass applying, selling data, scoring candidates for employers; scraping contacts, or building a job board, crawler, CRM or resume builder.
  • Employer-facing tools: employer packets and a viewer for them, posting checks for employers, feeding employer applicant systems, assurance levels and the "verified" rename, reference checks, credential imports.
  • Contradicts the repo, or too risky for a skill: the preview of how an AI screener reads your CV; brand-spoof and scam-template matching; legal explainers (unlawful requirements, reentry, AI-hiring laws) and licence-portability packs; links to agencies for reporting scams; a session-start AI-disclosure line; the plan's Go / Ask first / Skip labels, which would fork the existing decision vocabulary.
  • Niche once the audience is set aside: freelance client mode, benefit-claimant work-search logs, a federal resume format checker, occupation codes.
  • Not worth building, or already handled another way: follow-up notes as a skill; referral briefs (part of the deferred networking idea); a dashboard and pacing nudges; the cross-round consistency brief (covered by the debrief rule); Reactive Resume export or partnership; the form-fill extension and resume rendering; on-device models; hidden-character detection (an engine's job, not a skill's); the third-party code the plan itself says to avoid, such as JobSpy and Easy-Apply bots.

The rest

  • Covered: the core idea itself; questions to ask the employer; postings sent as screenshots; the dossier file; licence and credential requirements, with years of experience soft; your pay floor and other constraints; is-it-still-open checks with research on; career changers' transferable experience; resume readability for screening software; LinkedIn exports as input; interview prep and the "what may they ask" protections; routing tests for "is this legit".
  • Decided: MIT, so the plan's licence options aren't adopted, and agent/ stays reserved for AGPL; keep the name, a public repo, collaborators-only PRs; no fourth skill; the app, connector and other host surfaces deferred to L2; the agent never sends.
  • Yours to decide: the legal entity and copyright holder (LICENSE names moondocksaint while the repo sits in wavemanco); whether to retain a lawyer (defamation is the question nearest the scam-signal work); name clearance and trademark; directory listings; questions to Anthropic about its usage policy, which only matter at L2 and L3; who else can review and release; later: the agent's runtime, job-feed licences and telemetry.
  • Not applicable: target segments, languages and locales, institutions, funders, pricing (the paid hosting pass, the institution console), the competitor table, and the plan's method notes.
